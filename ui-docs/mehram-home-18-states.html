Every hero state from the specification, rendered in order of precedence. Resolve top to bottom, first true condition wins. Four photo states have been added — H16a, H16b, H16c and H17b — placing the photo before the proposal rather than after it.

The photo now comes before the proposal

Previously the photo unlocked after her wali approved. That was backwards on two counts: looking before proposing is encouraged in the sunnah, and no man pays PKR 4,000 to propose to a list of attributes.

New sequence: introduction card → he requests her photo → she decides → photos unlock for both at once → he proposes → her wali approves → chat opens with both walis.

The reveal is reciprocal, so she is never the only one exposed. Requests are capped at three a day. A declined request is silent — he is not told why and cannot ask again for 30 days. Screenshots are blocked throughout, which is standard on Pakistani matrimonial apps and expected by families.

There are four photo modes, chosen during onboarding and changeable any time (screen P4). If a user picks the most open one, her photo appears on the introduction card straight away and the button reads "Send proposal" — the request step is skipped entirely (state H16d). Default is the strictest mode for women and the open mode for men, matching how Pakistani matrimonial platforms already work.

Navigation — where each screen lives

The confusion between Home and Profile is a navigation problem, not a design one. Home is a tab. Profile is pushed on top of it. They never share a chrome.

Home (tab) shows a summary card only: age, city, sect, and five attribute rows. Its buttons act in place — tapping "Send proposal" sends the proposal and the card updates without leaving Home. Bottom nav always visible. No back button, ever.
Profile detail (pushed) is what opens when the user taps the card itself. Back arrow top left, title bar naming who they are looking at, bottom nav hidden, and the full record including family details and prompts. Same actions as the card, but a taller layout.
Back behaviour. Android hardware back and the on-screen arrow do the same thing: pop to Home. After an action completes on a pushed screen — proposal sent, photo requested — pop automatically and let Home reflect the new state. Never leave the user on a detail screen that no longer has a valid action.
